# Moderator guide and task script

## Moderator stance

Be neutral, quiet, and literal. Ask the participant to think aloud, but never teach the product's vocabulary or suggest the “right” answer. Wait at least ten seconds before intervening. When help is necessary, use the next level in order and record it:

1. `none`: no help;
2. `repeat_task`: repeat the task without rephrasing;
3. `neutral_prompt`: “What would you expect to do next?”;
4. `directional_hint`: name the relevant region or control;
5. `takeover`: demonstrate or complete an action.

`directional_hint` and `takeover` mean the task was not independently completed.

Do not defend surprising output. Say, “Tell me what you expected,” then observe. Do not promise that a missing capability will ship.

## Context questions

Ask before opening the product. Do not score these answers.

1. “Without naming confidential details, what recurring work process would you most like to improve?”
2. “What have you already tried with AI?”
3. “What would count as a useful result in 30 days?”
4. “What usually prevents a learning plan from fitting your week?”

## Exact tasks

### Task 1 — Explain the promise

Prompt: “Look at this page without clicking. In your own words, what do you think this will do, and what will it not do?”

Observe whether the participant identifies a personalized learning direction and notices that the build is text-only/private alpha. Do not point out labels.

### Task 2 — Start a path

Prompt: “Set up a path for the public-safe work process you described. Choose answers that are true for you.”

Observe goal choice, time, coding comfort, blocker, consent comprehension, and hesitation. If the default values are accepted without reading, ask only after completion: “How did you decide those values fit?”

### Task 3 — Complete the guided assessment

Prompt: “Continue until the product says it understands enough to review. Respond naturally; it is acceptable to say you do not know or have no example.”

Observe whether questions feel repetitive, leading, jargon-heavy, or unable to represent insufficient evidence. Record any answer the participant changes only because the interface seems to demand expertise.

### Task 4 — Correct the understanding

Prompt: “Review what the product believes. Change or remove anything that is inaccurate, overstated, unclear, or not yours.”

After the participant finishes, ask:

- “Was it obvious that you could disagree?”
- “Is anything still attributed to you without enough evidence?”

Count edits and removals; never copy their text into the coded observation form.

### Task 5 — Audit the report

Prompt: “Read the skill observations and recommendations as if you had received them without a moderator. Mark each finding supported, partly supported, materially wrong, or not assessable from what you said.”

For each materially wrong finding, record only the skill ID and bounded reason code. Ask: “What evidence would the product have needed?” Do not argue about the level.

### Task 6 — Judge the plan

Prompt: “Open the 30-day plan. Decide whether it fits your goal, experience, coding comfort, and available time. Then identify the first action you would actually take.”

Ask for 1–5 ratings:

- plan fit: `1` does not fit at all → `5` fits very well;
- report usefulness: `1` not useful → `5` very useful.

Then ask: “What one change would most improve the fit?” Code the answer; do not retain free-form content in the aggregate sheet.

### Task 7 — Data control

Prompt: “Without deleting anything yet, show where you would go to export or delete what this session created.”

Observe discovery and whether the participant understands browser-only versus durable state. If the participant chooses deletion, allow it and record the outcome. Never block a real deletion to preserve research data.

## Debrief

Ask in order:

1. “What felt most trustworthy?”
2. “What made you doubt the result?”
3. “Did the plan reflect anything beyond the goal category you selected?”
4. “Would speaking instead of typing materially improve your ability to answer? Why or why not?”
5. “Is there anything you want removed from the notes now?”

The voice question measures expected value, not permission to activate paid Realtime.

## Stop conditions

Stop immediately for withdrawn consent, sensitive data that cannot be safely removed, participant distress, a deletion failure involving retained research data, or a product behavior that could materially mislead the participant. Record a bounded stop code and escalate; do not continue to obtain a completion.
